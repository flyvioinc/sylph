# Voice & Copy Guidelines

## Brand Positioning

Flyvio is the open source Online Booking Tool (OBT) and airline NDC connector for Travel Management Companies — built so TMCs can finally extend, self-host, and tailor their booking stack instead of waiting on a vendor roadmap.

### Core Messaging Hierarchy

1. **Tagline:** The open source OBT for TMCs.
2. **One-liner:** Flyvio is the open source online booking tool for Travel Management Companies.
3. **Extended description:** Flyvio gives TMCs an OBT they actually own — open source, NDC-native, and built to be tailored per corporate client without a six-figure professional services bill. Pair it with the Flyvio NDC connector to pull airline content (fares, ancillaries, rich media) direct from carriers.

### Brand Beliefs

- TMCs deserve an OBT they can extend, not one they have to negotiate with a vendor to change.
- NDC is not a bolt-on. Modern corporate booking is built around it.
- Open source is the right answer for infrastructure that every TMC touches but no TMC should have to re-build.
- The booking experience is the product. If it's clunky, travelers book outside policy and the TMC loses.

## Voice Characteristics

### Tone Spectrum

| Attribute | We ARE | We are NOT |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Direct | Make the point in the first sentence | Burying the lede in setup |
| Technical | Use the real vocabulary — TMC, OBT, NDC, GDS, PNR, etc. | Dumbing down for general SaaS audiences |
| Practical | Concrete: "switch a corporate client to NDC content in a day, not a quarter" | Aspirational without specifics |
| Industry-aware | Talk like someone who's worked at or with a TMC | Outsider energy, "disrupting travel" hype |
| Confident | State what Flyvio is, not what it's not | Apologetic, hedged |

### Writing Rules

**Product name casing**
- Correct: "Flyvio is open source."
- Wrong: "flyvio is open source." / "FLYVIO is open source."

**Industry acronyms**
- TMC, OBT, NDC, GDS, PNR, ARC, BSP — written uppercase, no periods.
- Expand on first use in any external-facing piece: "Travel Management Companies (TMCs)…"
- Don't expand again in the same piece. The audience knows.

**Sentence structure**
- Short, declarative sentences. Fragments OK when they earn it.
- Use em dashes (—) for asides, not parentheses for asides.
- Oxford comma.

**Active voice**
- Yes: "Flyvio pulls NDC content direct from carriers."
- No: "NDC content is pulled direct from carriers by Flyvio."

### Word Choices

**Prefer:**
- "TMC" over "agency"
- "corporate traveler" over "business traveler" (the industry term)
- "travel arranger" over "booker" or "secretary"
- "trip" over "journey" or "itinerary" (use "itinerary" only when you mean the actual PNR/booking record)
- "content" over "inventory" when talking about airline fares/ancillaries (industry usage)
- "self-host" over "on-prem"
- "extend" over "customize"
- "ship" over "deliver"
- "open source" over "open-source" (no hyphen)

**Avoid:**
- "leverage", "utilize", "synergize", "empower"
- "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "next-generation", "revolutionary"
- "seamless", "robust", "world-class"
- "disrupt", "transform" (used as marketing verbs)
- "solution" as a noun for the product — Flyvio is a platform / an OBT / a connector
- "platform" used as a vague buzzword — only when it's literally the right word
- "AI" when it doesn't apply — Flyvio is a booking platform, not an AI product

## Copy Formats

### Headlines
- Short. 3–6 words ideal.
- Fragments fine.
- Lead with the noun ("The open source OBT") not the verb ("Book trips faster").

**Examples:**
- The open source OBT for TMCs.
- NDC, without the vendor lock-in.
- Your OBT. Your roadmap.

### Product descriptions
- Lead with what it does for a TMC, not what it is technically.
- One sentence = one idea.
- End with the operational benefit, not the feature.

**Pattern:** *[What the TMC can do] — [why that matters to their corporate client or P&L].*

### Feature copy
- Start with the verb.
- ≤15 words per line.
- No trailing periods on feature list items unless they're full sentences.

### Social media
- **LinkedIn:** the primary channel. Industry-aware posts for TMC ops/tech leaders and corporate travel buyers. Specific over general. Share opinions on the industry (NDC adoption, OBT lock-in, GDS economics), not announcements.
- **X/Twitter:** sharper, punchier, more dev-leaning when talking about the open source side.
- **Reddit:** r/travelagents and similar — talk like a peer, never marketing-speak.
- Exclamation marks: avoid. One per post max if absolutely needed.

### Email
- Subject lines: clear and specific. Never clickbait. The TMC audience deletes clickbait on sight.
- Body: conversational but purposeful. Open with the reason for the email in the first line.
- CTA: one clear action. Default to "reply" or "30-min call" — not "book a demo" until there's a demo to book.

## Describing Flyvio's capabilities

When writing about what Flyvio does, organize around these pillars:

1. **Open source OBT** — the booking interface and workflow engine TMCs license, self-host, and extend per corporate client.
2. **NDC connector** — direct airline integrations on the IATA NDC standard, so TMCs surface modern content (fares, ancillaries, rich media) without GDS-only limits.
3. **TMC-shaped, not enterprise-IT-shaped** — built around how TMCs actually configure clients, policies, and approval flows, not a generic B2B SaaS abstraction.
4. **Extensible by design** — TMCs add their own integrations, branding, and policy rules without waiting on a vendor.

Never position Flyvio as "an AI travel app" or "a consumer booking tool." Flyvio is corporate travel infrastructure for TMCs.
